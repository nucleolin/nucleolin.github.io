Research Vision

"How can we design tools, methods, and systems that make human cognition — and the richness of lived experience — legible to AI, so that humans and machines can genuinely co-create?"
I am fundamentally interested in the gap between how humans perceive and inhabit the world and how current AI systems represent it. Human experience is spatial, emotional, culturally situated, and deeply embodied — yet most AI operates on abstracted patterns stripped of this context. My work sits at the intersection of cognitive science, HCI, and AI.

This plays out across two interlocking threads. The first concerns built environments: architecture shapes how we feel, focus, and connect, yet designers still rely largely on intuition. I build systems that translate neuroimaging data, eye-tracking, and behavioral signals into design-time feedback — enabling evidence-based human–AI co-design. The second concerns social and informational landscapes: as conversational AI becomes a primary interface for knowledge-seeking, I examine how the psychological distance of a query shapes what users want from a system.
Human Experience → Design
Translating perceptual and neural signals into actionable design-time feedback.
Cognition → AI Evaluation
Using fMRI and behavioral data to benchmark AI representation alignment.
Social Context → Interfaces
Reshaping conversational AI design around situation-dependent information needs.

ArchSense: Multimodal Feedback System for Architectural Co-Design
Ongoing
02/2025 – present · NTHU HMI Lab
A real-time design feedback platform that fuses eye-tracking fixation maps, physiological signals (EDA, HR), and AI-predicted saliency overlays to help architects understand how users experience spatial proposals before they are built. Currently integrating a diffusion-model component for generative iteration on floor plans.

Ongoing
09/2024 – present · NUS AI4SG Lab (collab.)
Investigates how construal level theory and psychological distance (temporal, social, spatial, hypothetical) shape users' information needs when querying conversational AI. The current phase focuses on developing a distance-aware prompting framework that adapts LLM response style to query distance.

THEME 01
Human–AI Co-Design in Architecture
Evidence-Based Design Tools · HCI
Architecture shapes how we feel, focus, and connect — yet design decisions remain largely intuitive. I develop computational tools that feed real human perceptual data into the design process, enabling designers and AI to genuinely collaborate.
  • How can eye-tracking and physiological data be represented in ways designers can act on?
  • What level of AI autonomy is appropriate in a co-design workflow?
  • Can generative models produce spatially coherent proposals that satisfy measurable human perceptual criteria?

THEME 02
Neuroimaging & Spatial Cognition
fMRI · Scene Perception · Cognitive Maps
Using fMRI and behavioral paradigms to understand how the brain encodes spatial and architectural experience, and how these representations can ground better AI models of human perception.
  • Which cortical regions are preferentially activated by architectural versus natural scenes?
  • How do individual differences in spatial memory predict aesthetic preference?
  • Can scene-selective neural responses serve as evaluation signal for generative architectural AI?

THEME 03
Conversational AI & Information-Seeking
Psychological Distance · LLM UX
As LLMs become primary knowledge interfaces, I examine how psychological distance shapes information needs — and what it means to design AI that genuinely adapts to those needs rather than producing uniform responses.
  • How does temporal or social distance change what a user wants from a search system?
  • Can distance-aware prompting strategies measurably improve user satisfaction?
  • How do people calibrate trust in AI responses across different distance conditions?

THEME 04
NeuroAI: Brain-Inspired Machine Learning
Cognitive Maps · Shortcut Learning · Alignment
Using brain-imaging findings not just to study humans, but to probe and improve deep neural networks — asking whether alignment with biological cognition makes AI more robust and interpretable.
  • Do hippocampal cognitive map structures reduce shortcut learning in DNNs?
  • How does self-supervised learning in the cerebellum inform biologically plausible propagation?
  • Can neural alignment scores predict OOD generalization performance?
